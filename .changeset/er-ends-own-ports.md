ER diagrams give every relationship its own end where several lines meet one column. A table row can hold two line ends on a side with their bars or crow's feet apart; a third used to squeeze in between, so the marks ran together into one stroke and the reader could not tell which end belonged to which line. In a SaaS workspace schema, three relationships into `organizations.id` from the left merged that way. Now a row that holds more than two ends on a side grows taller, so each end has a port of its own on that row and the line still lands on the column it references. A one-row table with two ends on a side also makes room below them, clear of its corner. Lines that loop back to a table in the same column now take the port nearest the turn, which also removes the crossing the SaaS schema drew there. A table only grows when the drawing reads no worse: no more crossings, reader findings, detours or marks on labels, and no larger drawing.
