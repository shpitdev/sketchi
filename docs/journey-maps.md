# Journey maps

A user journey map draws what a persona does and how each step feels: stages as columns across the top, a card for each step under its stage, an emotion curve through a face per step at its sentiment score, and the pain points and opportunities found in each stage. It is a canonical family (`journey`), built with `sketchi.buildJourneyMap`, Studio's `build_journey_map`, `POST /api/v1/journey-maps/build`, `sketchi create`, or `generate --type journey`.

```json
{"type":"journey","spec":{"title":"Online purchase","persona":"Maya, a first-time buyer","stages":[{"name":"Discover","steps":[{"label":"Sees an ad","score":4,"touchpoint":"Instagram"}]},{"name":"Buy","steps":[{"label":"Creates an account","score":2,"touchpoint":"Website"},{"label":"Pays by card","score":3,"touchpoint":"Website"}],"painPoints":["Forced account creation"],"opportunities":["Offer guest checkout"]}]}}
```

## Contract

- `stages` run in order, each `{ name, steps, painPoints?, opportunities? }`. A stage has at least one step, and stage names are unique.
- A step has a `label`, a `score`, and optionally an `actor` (who acts; a step several actors share names them all, `"Customer, Agent"`) and a `touchpoint` (the channel: Email, Website, Phone).
- `score` is Mermaid's journey scale: a whole number from 1 (very negative) to 5 (very positive), 3 neutral.
- `persona` names whose journey it is. `actors`, when given, lists every actor a step names; a step naming any other actor is rejected.
- Steps and notes written apart from the stages (`steps: [{ stage, label, score }]`, `painPoints: [{ stage, text }]`) go under the stage each names; a step naming a new stage starts it. A step or note naming no stage, or a note naming a stage the journey lacks, is rejected with the stages it may name; nothing is dropped.
- A step several actors share names them all, `"Customer, Agent"`; against declared `actors`, a name holding a comma (`"Smith, Jane"`) stays whole.
- Code Mode and generation also read Mermaid's `sections` of `tasks` (and `Task: 5: Actor` strings), `title` for a stage's name, `task` for a step's label, `sentiment`, `emotion` or `rating` for its score (numeric strings too), `channel` for its touchpoint, a list of actors (Mermaid's `Task: 4: Customer, Agent` too) as one shared actor, `pains` and `ideas` for a stage's notes, a persona written as `{ name, description }`, and flat `steps` that each name their `stage`.
- Validation rejects a score off the scale or not whole, an empty or repeated stage, an actor outside `actors`, repeated actors, more than 10 stages, 40 steps, 8 steps in a stage, 4 pain points or opportunities in a stage or 8 actors, and text over its limit (step labels 90 characters, notes 100, stage names 40, actors and touchpoints 32, the persona 80). Each rejection carries a repair hint.

## Layout

- The title, then `Persona:` and `Actors:` lines, sit above the map. A header column names each row: Steps, Experience, Pain points, Opportunities.
- Each stage is a colored header over its steps' columns, never narrower than the widest word of its name or notes; a stage wider than its steps centers them. Stage colors cycle through blue, violet and pink, so none reads as a pain point, an opportunity or a mood. Dividers part the stages below their headers.
- Every card of a journey shares one width, the narrowest at which no label takes more lines than on the widest card, and every card of a band one height. A step's label is the card's own text, at its top; its actor and touchpoint sit at its foot, on one line as `actor · touchpoint` when that fits and each on its own lines otherwise. Labels wrap whole, never mid-word.
- The experience row is a scale: a label per level at its left (`5`, `4`, `3 · Neutral`, `2`, `1`), a faint rule at every level but neutral's dashed baseline, and a face per step, centered under its card, exactly on its score's level, with the score printed just above it, clear of the curve, the face and the level above. A face's mouth smiles above neutral, sets flat on it and frowns below it, and its color runs red through gray to green, so the score reads without color. One rounded line runs through every face of a band, behind them.
- A stage's pain points and opportunities stack as notes as wide as its columns, in rows beneath the curve. A row appears only in bands that hold a note of its kind.
- A journey too wide to read at 2.45 to 1 first narrows its cards, while every label keeps to four lines, then raises its curve a step at a time. Past that it wraps into bands of whole stages, one under another, the widest band as narrow as it can be, then the most even, earlier bands fuller. The curve runs on across a wrap: dashed out of a band toward the next step's score and into the next band from the last one's, whose Experience label reads continued. A stage too long for any band splits at a band's end and repeats its header, marked `(continued)`; its notes stay under its first part. Every placement fits the CLI's PNG ceiling.
- Faces export as grouped Excalidraw ellipses with eyes and a rounded mouth line, under the `journey-mark` renderer role; each card groups with its details.

## Scenarios

Maintained scenarios, from Frontierclimb's eval draft: `online-purchase-journey` (smoke: four stages, touchpoints), `saas-onboarding-journey` (challenge: six uneven stages, long labels, five actors) and `support-ticket-journey` (standard). Their `--input` checks score the model's own spec: `persona`, `stage:<key>` and `stage-order`, `step:<key>` under its stage and `step-order:<stage>`, `score:<key>` exactly, `actor:<key>` or `touchpoint:<key>` (the field, or a label opening with it), `pain-point:<stage>:<n>` and `opportunity:<stage>:<n>` under their stage in any order, `steps-exact`, `pain-points-exact`, `opportunities-exact`, and `actors-stated` where the prompt names actors. A label may drop the actor or persona its step names, or a leading article. `journey-drawn` reads the scene back: every stage header in order, each card under its stage with its label, a face at its score's height inside its step's column with the matching expression, the curve through the faces, and each note in its stage's column. `journey-layout` checks that nothing overlaps, every text fits its box and every row keeps to its stages.

The fuzz suite (`pnpm nx run diagram-excalidraw:journey-fuzz`, CI runs 8) draws 900 random journeys across three seeds: one step to forty, one stage to ten, short, long, hyphenated and Japanese text, actors and touchpoints, and empty or full note rows. Each must export and reload clean, stay under the canvas's 600 elements, pass every layout rule (no text crossed by the curve or a rule, each printed score matching its face) and read at 2.45 to 1 or less. Each fits the CLI's PNG ceiling too, but for the rarest 30-plus-step journeys with full note stacks (6 of 900, capped at 1 in 100); Excalidraw and hosted PNGs are unaffected.
