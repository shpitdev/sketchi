# Class diagrams

A class diagram draws an object model: classes, interfaces and enums with their attributes and methods, and the UML relationships between them, each with its own line and head. It is a canonical family (`class`), built with `sketchi.buildClassDiagram`, Studio's `build_class_diagram`, `POST /api/v1/class-diagrams/build`, `sketchi create`, or `generate --type class`.

```json
{"type":"class","spec":{"title":"Shapes","classes":[{"id":"Shape","kind":"abstract","attributes":["# x: double"],"methods":["+ area(): double {abstract}"]},{"id":"Circle","attributes":["- radius: double"],"methods":["+ area(): double"]},{"id":"Canvas","methods":["+ add(s: Shape): void"]}],"relationships":[{"kind":"inheritance","from":"Circle","to":"Shape"},{"kind":"aggregation","from":"Canvas","to":"Shape","fromMultiplicity":"1","toMultiplicity":"0..*","toRole":"shapes"}]}}
```

## Contract

- A class has a unique `id` (no colon or space), an optional `name` (the id when omitted), an optional `kind` (`class`, `abstract`, `interface` or `enum`), an optional `stereotype` of its own, `attributes` and `methods`. An enum lists its values as attributes, and Code Mode and generation also read them from `values`.
- An attribute has a `name`, an optional `type`, a `visibility` (`public`, `private`, `protected`, `package`) and `static`. A method has a `name`, `params` (`{ name, type? }`), an optional `returns`, a `visibility`, `static` and `abstract`. Code Mode and generation also read a member written on one line as UML writes it: `"- radius: double"`, `"+ count: int {static}"`, `"+ draw(ctx: Canvas): void {abstract}"`, with `+`, `-`, `#`, `~` or a visibility word in front and `{static}`, `{abstract}`, `static` or `abstract` as markers.
- A relationship has a `kind`, `from` and `to`, and an optional `label`. `inheritance`: `from` extends `to`. `realization`: `from` implements the interface `to`. `composition` and `aggregation`: `from` is the whole, `to` the part. `association`: the two know each other. `dependency`: `from` uses `to`. Code Mode and generation read `extends`, `implements`, `uses` and the like as kinds, and `source`/`target` for `from`/`to`.
- Associations, compositions and aggregations carry `navigable` (an open arrow at `to`), `fromMultiplicity`, `toMultiplicity` (`1`, `0..1`, `*`, `1..*`, `2..4`; `many` and `0..n` read as UML's), `fromRole` and `toRole`. Other kinds carry none, and a composition's whole is `1` or `0..1`.
- An interface extends only interfaces, a class implements an interface rather than extending it, a realization targets an interface, and no class extends itself through any chain. At most 30 classes, 30 members per class, 300 members and 60 relationships, names of at most 64 characters and members of at most 96. Each rejection carries a repair hint.

## Layout

- Classes stand in rows: every superclass and interface in a row above the classes that extend or implement it, other related classes side by side where a row has room. A local search balances that against rows no wider, and a layout no taller, than about 1.6 to 1 allows. A candidate keeps every composition's or aggregation's whole above its part, and the renderer keeps it unless another placement routes clearly better.
- A row never lines up a ring of related classes side by side, since one of its links would have to reach around a box; one class moves to the next row instead.
- Rows are ordered to cut crossings (barycenter sweeps, then swaps of neighbors), counting each box that stands between two classes side by side as a crossing. Classes then slide along their rows so each superclass centers over its subclasses and siblings line up beneath it, and a box at either end of a row with no line to another row closes up on its nearest neighbor that has one, so no row trails off past an empty gap. A relationship that skips a row holds a corridor between the boxes of each row it passes.
- Lines between rows leave the bottom of the upper class and reach the top of the lower one; lines between neighbors in a row join their facing sides; a line between two classes of one row with another box between them runs through the channel above or below that row, whichever it crosses fewer lines in; a relationship from a class to itself loops out and back on its freer side.
- A box with more ends on a side than it holds a port step apart grows to hold them; ports never pack closer.
- Corridors from a parent through the row of its other children spread one to a gap, so many subclasses wrapped onto a second row each drop straight through a gap of their own.
- Every end has a port of its own, spread along its side in the order its lines head and as near as that allows to its partner, so most lines between rows run straight. A line that would jog only a little runs straight when either end can move onto the other's line, and no port sits on a compartment's divider.
- Neighboring lines stand at least the gap a reader tells two lines apart by (24 px at the export's size): ports on one side, tracks in a channel, and the lines of two rows across their channel, a corridor included. A side too short for its labels gives up label room before its step.
- Each line that steps sideways between two rows does so on a track of its own in the channel between them, the tracks ordered to cross least, so no two lines share or hug a run. Every line keeps a straight run of at least 50 units at each end, room for Excalidraw's arrow, triangle or diamond at full size.
- Several placements are routed in full (wholes above parts, the plain rows, the same aiming nearer square, and one keeping related classes side by side; models over 20 classes try the first two), and the renderer keeps the one with the fewest crossings, reader findings and detours at a readable aspect.
- An export that still reads past 2.4:1 (measured as the hosted PNG frames it) tries one more placement, in which a superclass moves together with every class under it, so a whole hierarchy can step a row down beside another. A placement within 2.4:1 replaces one past it only when it draws no more crossings, reader findings, detours or hugging lines, and no longer a long side. Observer-pattern's two interfaces go from one row at 2.6:1 to a staircase at 1.3:1: Subject above WeatherStation, Observer beside it and the displays below.

## Excalidraw

Each class is a group: a square-cornered outline (the shape every relationship binds to), a name band with its `«interface»`, `«abstract»`, `«enumeration»` or own stereotype line over the name as bound text, a methods compartment whose top is the divider, and one multi-line text per compartment at 22 units a row. All three shapes carry the `class-box` role. Excalidraw cannot draw italics or underlines, so abstract classes show their keyword and abstract or static members end in `{abstract}` or `{static}`.

Relationships are elbow arrows bound to their classes at fixed points, so moving a class keeps each end on its port. Heads are Excalidraw's own: `triangle_outline` for inheritance and realization, `diamond` and `diamond_outline` at the whole of a composition and an aggregation, and `arrow` for a navigable end and a dependency, with realization and dependency dashed. Multiplicities and roles sit beside their end's first run, on opposite sides of the line, grouped with their class; the room they need beside a line between neighbors is held clear while the lines route. A relationship's name sits beside its longest run, wrapping onto up to five balanced lines when one has no room. Every label is placed clear of every box, line and other label.

## Scenarios

Maintained scenarios: `shape-hierarchy-class` (an interface realized by an abstract base, three subclasses and an aggregation), `online-shop-domain-class` (associations with multiplicities and a name, a composition, an aggregation, an abstract payment with two subclasses and an enum), `observer-pattern-class` (two interfaces realized by three classes, a navigable association with a role and a named dependency) and `library-system-class` (fifteen classes, sixteen relationships). Their `--input` checks require every stated class and its kind, every attribute with its type, visibility and static marker, every method with its parameter and return types and markers, no class or relationship the prompt does not state, and every relationship between the right classes, of the right kind and direction, with its multiplicities, roles and navigability. A drawn check confirms the scene shows every member, head, dash, multiplicity and role, and a clearance check that every label stands clear.
