# Native Gantt charts

Use `gantt` for project plans and schedules: tasks with start dates and durations or end dates, finish-to-start dependencies, milestones and phases. Resource loading, critical-path analysis and working-day calendars are out of scope. Use `timeline` for chronologies without durations or dependencies.

```json
{"type":"gantt","spec":{"title":"Release plan","tasks":[{"id":"design","label":"Design","start":"2026-03-02","end":"2026-03-06","phase":"Build"},{"id":"api","label":"API","start":"2026-03-09","duration":10,"phase":"Build","dependsOn":["design"],"progress":40},{"id":"release","label":"Release","start":"2026-03-30","milestone":true,"phase":"Launch","dependsOn":["api"]}]}}
```

Pass this document to `sketchi create --json`, or its `spec` to `sketchi.buildGantt({ spec, options: { artifactFormats: ["scene", "excalidraw", "png"] } })`. Public HTTP builds use `POST /api/v1/gantts/build`; generation accepts `type: "gantt"`. Studio exposes `build_gantt`.

## Contract

Every task needs a unique `id`, a `label` and a `start` day (`YYYY-MM-DD`), plus exactly one of:

- `end`: the last day, inclusive. `"start":"2026-03-02","end":"2026-03-06"` is five days.
- `duration`: whole calendar days counting `start`, so `"duration":5` ends on `start` + 4. Pass a stated duration as `duration` rather than computing `end`.
- `milestone: true` (or `duration: 0`): a single day, drawn as a diamond. It takes no `end` other than its own day.

Optional fields:

- `dependsOn`: ids of the tasks that must finish before this one starts (finish-to-start).
- `phase`: the header a task sits under. When any task has a phase, every task needs one. Top-level `phases` sets their order (default: first use); at most 12.
- `progress`: percent complete, 0–100, on tasks but not milestones.
- `today`: draws a Today line on that day. Nothing reads the clock, so output stays deterministic.

Unknown or self dependencies, dependency cycles, an `end` before `start`, both or neither of `end` and `duration`, fractional durations, unparseable dates, partial phases, more than 60 tasks or 120 dependencies, and plans spanning more than a century (36,525 days, Today included) are rejected with repair hints. No field takes coordinates. A task that starts before a predecessor finishes still builds, with a `gantt_schedule_conflict` warning that names the predecessor's last day, so computed dates can be checked against the prompt.

## Rendering

The renderer owns every position:

- **Axis.** One time scale runs across the plot, which spreads toward 960 px; a short chart narrows it, down to 640 px, to stay no wider than 2.2 times its height. Ticks are the finest of days, weeks (Mondays), months, quarters, or 1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 25 or 50 years whose labels all fit their cells, with at most 36 cells; half-century ticks fit any valid plan on the narrowest plot, so tick labels never overlap. A band above them names the month (over days) or year. Month and quarter axes add unlabeled weekly or monthly gridlines. The axis starts and ends on tick boundaries around the plan, and charts with 16 or more tasks repeat it below their rows.
- **Rows.** Each task gets its own row, in input order, under its phase's header. Bars span their days exactly: a bar starts on its first day's left boundary and ends on its last day's right boundary, and draws at least 2 px wide, so a one-day task on a multi-year axis stays visible. A milestone's diamond and the Today line sit in the middle of their day.
- **Table.** A table left of the plot names every row, so the plot holds only bars, diamonds and the dependencies between them. A name column gives each task's name, indented under its phase's header and wrapped at word boundaries past 260 px; names are never cut. A date column ending just before the plot gives each task's dates and progress (`Mar 9–18 · 40%`), right-aligned, in the timeline's caption style. A phase's header row gives its name and, in the date column, its span. A light rule runs between every two rows, from the table's left edge across the plot, so each name reads against its bar.
- **Progress.** The completed part of a bar fills a darker shade of its color, and the date column states the percent.
- **Dependencies.** Each one is an elbow arrow from its predecessor's end into its successor's start: down a gutter between rows and in from the left, or, when the successor starts before the predecessor ends, down into the gutter above the successor and back to its start. Routes are the cheapest orthogonal paths: they keep clear of every bar (8 px beside one), bend as little as they can, and charge for every pixel behind the predecessor's start, so a route doubles back only around something a forward route would detour far to pass. A turn after a run shorter than 28 px reads as a kink and costs extra, and so does running within 7 px along a bar's top or bottom edge. A route is found without kink costs first; one that kinks searches the same grid again under them, for the cheapest route once kinks are paid, on a budget of its own so weighing kinks never costs another route its search. A bar may be left from its right end, unless it is narrower than 8 px, where that end sits beside its arrivals' arrowheads. Exits near a bar's end line up with the successor's top entries, so a successor starting under its predecessor's end takes a straight drop, with a point 19 px before its side, so a close successor takes one elbow rather than a step before its arrowhead, and with the clearance beside other bars, so a route passing one drops straight past it. A diamond offers points on its lower and upper edges in line with the same columns. No route passes another's arrowhead, nor crosses the last 40 px of a side entry; crossing any other last run costs four times a plain crossing. Routes from different predecessors into different successors never run within 12 px of each other without paying for it, and never lie on one another. Routes out of one predecessor may leave it down one trunk, sharing their first run and branching off past each other's turns but never through an arrowhead, so a fan-out draws without crossings. Routes into one successor arrive side by side, each at its own point, at least 7 px apart and off the Today line; a bar awaiting more than one is entered from the left above and below its middle, the upper entry first from above; a bar's first exits and a milestone's right half stay free for the dependencies leaving it. Each one ends in a run of fixed length, so arrowheads draw at one size. A task keeps as many side entries free as it still awaits dependencies, and its own departures keep off them too; a side entry is never taken where its arrowhead would meet the task's own exit. When the usual entries are taken, a successor is entered anywhere along its outline that keeps clear, and only then may arrowheads crowd. After routing, arrivals into one successor that detour, cross or conflict are tried in the other order and, into a milestone, with the entries above and below its left vertex first; a plan is kept only with fewer conflicts or, tying, a shorter drawing with fewer bends, kinks and crossings. Then each route that passes another's arrowhead or lies along it is rerouted, or the other one is, whichever leaves the pair with the fewest conflicts and the shortest drawing; repairs draw on their own search budget. A chart left with a few such conflicts is laid out again with roomier rows, whose wider gutters hold more lanes, and the layout with the fewest is kept. The axis starts 24 px into the plot, leaving a corridor that holds no bars or text, and further when a milestone in its first days would reach into it. When a crowded plan leaves a dependency no route within the router's budget, it runs into the gutter beside its predecessor, along that corridor and its successor's row, and into the successor from the left, at the offsets that keep furthest from other routes and arrowheads (its first run may lie beside its siblings' as they leave the predecessor), so it is still drawn, crosses nothing and never lies on another route. Routing work is bounded and deterministic: a route searches at most 12,000 steps, a chart at most 6,000 per dependency and 160,000 in all, repairs 48,000 more, weighing kinks 1,500 per dependency and 48,000 more, and a chart is laid out at most three times; reordering stops once the chart's budget is spent; `layoutGanttDiagram` reports the work a layout took, and tests bound it instead of timing it.
- Gridlines and axis lines break behind text. The Today line also breaks behind every bar and diamond, so it never meets a label. A scene holds at most 600 elements, counted with every break, so a crowded chart keeps every tick's gridline only while they fit, then drops the weekly or monthly minor lines, the repeated bottom axis, and the gridlines inside month or year bands; only then does Today stop breaking behind bars.
- Phases take colors in the chart family's fixed categorical order; milestones are ink. Bars and diamonds carry the `gantt-mark` renderer role and export at roughness 0. Bars keep square corners. In Excalidraw, a task's bar and progress strip are one group and its name and dates another, and dependencies are bound to their bars: dragging a bar to new dates carries its dependencies and leaves the table in place.

## Scenarios

Maintained CLI scenarios: `app-release-gantt` (a software release with dependencies, two milestones and progress), `cafe-fit-out-gantt` (a construction plan in four phases with a Today line), and `data-platform-migration-gantt` (28 tasks in six phases over seven months). Their `--input` checks use the model's own spec. They require every task by label, its start and last day or milestone day, its phase and progress, every stated dependency, no invented dependency, phase order, Today, and an exact task count. `gantt-geometry` reads the scene back: every bar and diamond must sit on its dates within 0.02 days, as measured from the axis gridlines. The gridlines must cover the plan, and every dependency must be drawn. `gantt-drawn-data` grades what is drawn: every prompt task's label, its date caption as the renderer formats it, its diamond or bar, its phase header in prompt order, every stated dependency arrow, and the Today line and label. `gantt-clearance` fails text that overlaps other text or a mark, any dependency that crosses a mark or text, passes within 4 px of another's arrowhead, or runs within 2 px of another route (except side by side out of one predecessor or into one successor), a dependency that loops back behind the one predecessor of the next row's task starting after it (unless that predecessor is a sliver under 12 px with four or more dependencies arriving or leaving), and any rule that crosses text. Fixture proof is deterministic, not model-quality evidence.
