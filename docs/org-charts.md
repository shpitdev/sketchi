# Org charts

An org chart draws who reports to whom: a card for each person or position with their name, title and department, each manager above the reports they lead, assistants beside their manager, and dotted-line (matrix) reports as secondary lines. It is a canonical family (`org-chart`), built with `sketchi.buildOrgChart`, Studio's `build_org_chart`, `POST /api/v1/org-charts/build`, `sketchi create`, or `generate --type org-chart`.

```json
{"type":"org-chart","spec":{"title":"Startup team","people":[{"id":"ceo","name":"Ada Lovelace","title":"CEO"},{"id":"ea","name":"Grace Hopper","title":"Executive Assistant","manager":"ceo","assistant":true},{"id":"cto","name":"Alan Turing","title":"CTO","department":"Engineering","manager":"ceo"},{"id":"dev","name":"Ken Thompson","title":"Engineer","department":"Engineering","manager":"cto"},{"id":"cpo","name":"Radia Perlman","title":"CPO","department":"Product","manager":"ceo"}],"dottedLines":[{"report":"dev","manager":"cpo"}]}}
```

## Contract

- A person has a unique `id` (no colon), a `name`, an optional `title` and `department`, and `manager`: the id of the person they report to. Only the top person has no manager; `forest: true` draws several trees side by side, each with a top of its own.
- `assistant: true` draws an assistant beside their manager, off the reporting line; an assistant has no reports. `avatar: true` draws an initials placeholder for a photo.
- `dottedLines: [{ id, report, manager, label? }]` are secondary reports: `report` also answers to `manager` outside the solid line. A dotted line joins two different people and never repeats a solid one. `label` carries the scope or reason the request states for it, such as "for clinical systems". A single dotted line written as an object reads as a list of one.
- `groupByDepartment: true` draws each department below the top as a labeled region.
- Code Mode and generation also read `reportsTo`, `managerId` and `parent` for `manager`, `role`, `position` and `jobTitle` for `title`, `team` for `department`, `kind: "assistant"`, a person's `directReports` (ids), explicit `reportingLines: [{ manager, report }]`, and dotted lines written on a person (`dottedLineTo: "cfo"`) or as `{ from, to }` (from reports to to). A dotted line without an id takes `<report>-dotted-<manager>`.
- Validation rejects an unknown manager, a person managing themselves, a reporting cycle, more than one top person without `forest`, an assistant without a manager or with reports, a malformed or repeated dotted line, a colon in an id, repeated ids, names over 60 characters and titles over 80, chains deeper than 12 levels, and more than 100 people or 24 dotted lines. Each rejection carries a repair hint.

## Layout

- The tree reads top-down. Each manager's reports hang from a bus: a stem down from the manager's card, a line across, and a drop into each report's top. Lines that leave one card at one point share their trunk until they part, drawn once.
- Cards share one width per level, and the reports of one manager one height, so a row or a stack reads as one rank. Names and titles wrap whole onto balanced lines, never cut.
- Five or more leaf reports (no reports or assistants of their own) stack under their manager: one column beside a spine near the manager's left edge, or, from five, two columns either side of the stem in reading order. A stack holds at most twenty; more split into stacks side by side. A manager whose reports mix leaves and managers stacks the leaves as one item in the row.
- Assistants hang off their manager's stem, right then left (right only beside a one-column spine), between the manager and the reports.
- Peers keep their rank. A placement whose export reads wider than 2.4 to 1, or would not fit the CLI's PNG, narrows from the bottom up: it first stacks every group of two or more leaves, then stacks up to eight leaves in one column, then lists the widest small teams (a manager whose reports lead only leaves) along their manager's stem, each report with its team indented under it, in two columns and then one. The top person and their direct reports always keep their rows.
- Only when that is not enough does a row wrap, rows below the top before the top's own: the row hangs from a trunk, splits evenly either side of it, and the last part centers under it; of the rows no single subtree dominates, those that would narrow most, and the rows down the chain of widest subtrees. Wraps a later wrap made unnecessary are undone. Twelve vice presidents under one chief are the case that needs it.
- With `groupByDepartment`, a person with reports whose department differs from their manager's starts a region around their whole subtree. Regions never nest, keep room beside them for a line to pass, and keep their name clear of the line that drops in. Inside a region of its own department a card leaves the department to the region's name; elsewhere a department shows where it starts, and the card's color carries it below. A chart that names no department colors each branch below the top.
- Dotted lines route orthogonally clear of every card, of regions neither end sits in and of every region name, never along a solid line, an earlier dotted line or a region's outline, and a line between two people of one region stays inside it. A line that would cross the hierarchy searches the whole chart for a way around it, and takes it when the detour costs less than the crossings it saves (a crossing weighs 500 units of length); a chart whose detours would leave a later line without a route draws every line the plain way. A line across the whole chart goes over it on a lane of its own, above every other line; one no search can place draws straight under the cards, the only diagonal line a chart can hold.
- A label sits on its line: beside the run it fits beside nearest the line, on up to three balanced lines, clear of cards, lines, regions and other labels. It never covers a card or a region name: where no spot beside its route is clear, the line goes over the chart, whose top run always has room, and past that the label sits above the chart over its line's start. The layout readout reports any label over a card (`label-over-card`), and the fuzz holds it to none.
- Work is bounded: a chart of 80 people tries 15 placements, and dotted lines share a fixed search budget.

## Excalidraw

Each card is a group (`person:<id>`): a rounded rectangle in its department's color, the title and department as text bound to its bottom, the name as text above it, and an avatar ellipse when asked. Reporting lines are headless elbow arrows bound to both cards, drawn under them; dotted lines are dashed, in their own color, with an arrowhead at the second manager. Regions are Excalidraw frames named for their department, holding their cards and the lines between them. Every export validates before and after a save and reload.

## Scenarios

Maintained scenarios: `startup-team-org-chart` (twelve people under a CEO and three leads), `engineering-department-org-chart` (twenty-five people over three levels, a director with eight engineers and a staff engineer beside the directors), `leadership-dotted-line-org-chart` (an executive assistant and a director's dotted-line report to another chief) and `sales-regions-org-chart` (fourteen people in three department regions). Their `--input` checks require every stated person by name with their title and department, the manager each reports to (none for the top), every stated assistant and dotted line, no person, assistant or dotted line the prompt does not state, and department regions when asked. A stated scope or reason for a dotted line must appear in its label (`dotted-line-scope:<n>`; the leadership scenario's "for clinical systems"). A drawn check confirms every card, name, title, reporting line, dotted line, dotted-line label and region, and a layout check that cards stand apart and lines run orthogonally between cards, through none and never crossing.

## Known limits

- A random tree of 60 to 100 people with long titles may export larger than the CLI's 6.3M-square-unit PNG ceiling; realistic charts (the 80-person stress fixture is 2774×1492) fit, and Excalidraw export is unaffected.
- On rare random shapes (10 of 900 random charts in the local fuzz, none with a defect in its export, cards, reporting lines or labels, and none in the same 900 charts with every dotted line labeled), a chart above fifty people reads wider than 2.5 to 1 (up to 3.1), a dotted line falls back to a straight line or runs along a region's outline, or a dotted label touches a region outline.
- A dotted line out of a stacked report may still bend once beside its manager's card where a wider card below leaves no straight channel.
